# Journal Evaluation

## Goal

Decide whether a review invitation is worth the user's attention without maintaining a local whitelist.

## Core Rule

- Clearly reputable and well-known journals: keep
- Unfamiliar, weak, spammy, or hard-to-verify journals: ignore

## Practical Heuristic

Use model knowledge first.

Strong positive signals:

- widely known flagship journals or established specialty journals
- major publishers or societies with recognizable editorial programs
- professional editorial email domain and manuscript workflow
- language consistent with normal peer review rather than bulk solicitation

Strong negative signals:

- vague or generic journal branding
- spam-like wording, urgency, or poor grammar
- suspicious sender domain unrelated to a real publisher
- topic mismatch with the manuscript or the user's field
- obviously predatory or low-credibility presentation

## When To Browse

Browse only when the journal is not obviously good or obviously bad.

Prefer:

- official journal homepage
- publisher journal page
- editorial board or aims-and-scope page

Avoid treating third-party listicles or SEO spam pages as authoritative.

## Decision Standard

If after a quick check the journal still does not look clearly reputable, classify it as not worth attention.

This skill is intentionally conservative.
